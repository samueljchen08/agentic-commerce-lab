# 0. EXECUTIVE DIRECTIVE

## 0.1 What this document changes

v2.0 correctly narrowed the company to a merchant-side decision system for agent-mediated commerce and correctly removed most SaaS infrastructure from the critical path. It still defined the MVP too broadly.

This version splits the build into three concrete releases:

1. **Vertical Slice** — functional end-to-end system in approximately 2–3 focused days.
2. **Demo MVP** — compelling external product demo in approximately 7–10 focused days.
3. **Validated MVP** — research-grade validation layer over the next 2–4 weeks.

The coding agent must optimize for this sequence.

The immediate goal is **not** to prove that the laboratory perfectly predicts live ChatGPT, Gemini, Shopify, or other agent surfaces.

The immediate goal is to demonstrate the full commercial mechanism:

> merchant-controlled intervention  
> → measured change in agent choice  
> → modeled order impact  
> → merchant-wide contribution economics  
> → ranked action  
> → sensitivity / breakeven analysis

The MVP succeeds when a viewer can understand why an apparently attractive agent-optimization action can destroy value and why another, less obvious intervention is economically superior.

The validated MVP then establishes whether the measurement instrument is stable enough to justify merchant pilots.

# PART I — COMPANY THESIS

## 1. What the product is

Agentic Commerce Lab is a **merchant-side decision system for agent-mediated demand**.

It estimates how buyer agents respond to merchant-controlled commercial and product-data variables, then combines those measured response effects with merchant economics to answer:

> **Which specific change should the merchant make to maximize incremental contribution profit from agent-mediated demand?**

The product is not primarily:

- an AI visibility tracker;
- a share-of-voice dashboard;
- a prompt-monitoring product;
- a synthetic-shopper simulator;
- a generic feed optimizer;
- a catalog-quality score;
- a recommendation report generated by an LLM.

Its durable product loop is:

```text
buyer intent
    ↓
candidate discovery / retrieval
    ↓
agent consideration
    ↓
controlled intervention experiment
    ↓
agent choice elasticity
    ↓
merchant economics
    ↓
ranked intervention
    ↓
merchant execution
    ↓
realized outcome
    ↓
lab → live calibration data
```

The initial MVP implements the center of this loop:

```text
controlled intervention
    ↓
agent choice
    ↓
merchant economics
    ↓
ranked intervention
```

---

## 2. The wedge

Existing AI-commerce and AI-search products generally answer questions such as:

- Does the brand appear?
- Is the product retrieved?
- How often is it recommended?
- Are product attributes complete?
- Which prompts mention the merchant?
- How should content be phrased?

Agentic Commerce Lab must answer a more economically consequential question:

> **Given a fixed competitive set and a buyer mandate, how responsive is agent choice to each merchant-controlled intervention, and which intervention creates the most incremental contribution after all merchant-wide costs are included?**

The wedge therefore has two parts:

### 2.1 Proprietary measurement

Measure **agent demand elasticity** with respect to merchant-controllable variables:

- price;
- shipping cost;
- shipping SLA;
- return terms;
- structured attributes;
- bundles;
- availability;
- descriptions / product representation;
- agent-specific offers where operationally feasible.

### 2.2 Economic translation

Translate model-measured choice effects into merchant decisions using:

- contribution margin;
- post-selection conversion;
- agent-channel volume;
- channel-wide exposure;
- cannibalization;
- return effects;
- fulfillment costs;
- price elasticity;
- operational feasibility.

The economic engine by itself is not the moat.

The experimental effect data by itself is not the product.

The combination is the commercial wedge.

---

## 3. Long-term venture-scale thesis

The company becomes venture-scale only if it progresses beyond “run LLM experiments and produce a report.”

The intended compounding asset is:

```text
merchant intervention
    × product/category state
    × buyer intent
    × agent/provider/surface
    → laboratory choice effect
    → live choice / conversion effect
    → realized merchant economics
```

Over time, the platform should accumulate structured tuples of:

```text
(context, intervention, lab effect, live effect, economic outcome)
```

This creates the possibility of:

1. **category-specific priors** — what changes tend to matter for luggage vs electronics vs beauty;
2. **agent-specific response models** — what ChatGPT-like surfaces respond to versus Gemini-like surfaces;
3. **lab→live calibration curves** — which laboratory effects reliably transfer;
4. **intervention recommendation without exhaustive testing** — eventually predicting promising arms before running them;
5. **merchant-specific continuous optimization** — experimentation becomes an operating system rather than a one-time audit;
6. **execution infrastructure** — controlled feed, offer, bundle, and policy changes can later be pushed through merchant systems;
7. **cross-merchant learning** — priors improve while raw merchant economics remain private.

The defensibility claim must therefore be:

> **The moat is not the simulator. The moat is the accumulated calibrated mapping from merchant-controlled interventions to realized agent-mediated economics.**

If the company never reaches calibration and merchant execution, it risks becoming a feature.

# PART II — CRITICAL CORRECTIONS FROM v2.0

## 4. Correction A — selection is not an order

v2.0 correctly distinguished agent comparison opportunities from agent orders but did not fully separate the two in the value bridge.

The laboratory directly measures:

```text
P(select focal product | mandate, candidate set, intervention, provider)
```

It does **not** directly measure:

```text
P(order | selected)
```

Therefore the economic engine must include post-selection conversion explicitly.

### 4.1 Required notation

For intervention `a`:

```text
N_cmp
    annual relevant agent comparison events

s_a
    P(select focal product | intervention a)

q_a
    P(order | focal product selected, intervention a)

CM_a
    contribution margin per resulting focal-product order

κ
    cannibalization fraction:
    fraction of incremental agent-attributed orders that would have
    occurred through another merchant-owned channel anyway

CM_displaced
    contribution margin of the displaced alternative channel/order
```

Then:

```text
agent_orders(a)
    = N_cmp × s_a × q_a
```

and:

```text
gross_agent_contribution(a)
    = agent_orders(a) × CM_a
```

The incremental contribution from measured agent demand is:

```text
Δgross_agent(a)
    = N_cmp × [s_a × q_a × CM_a
               - s_0 × q_0 × CM_0]
```

Cannibalization adjustment:

```text
incremental_agent_orders(a)
    = N_cmp × [s_a × q_a - s_0 × q_0]

cannibalization_cost(a)
    = max(0, incremental_agent_orders(a))
      × κ
      × CM_displaced
```

Then:

```text
agent_channel_gain(a)
    = Δgross_agent(a)
      - cannibalization_cost(a)
```

The total objective remains:

```text
Δtotal_contribution(a)
    = agent_channel_gain(a)
      + non_agent_effect(a)
```

where `non_agent_effect(a)` captures the merchant-wide economics of interventions that cannot be restricted to agent traffic.

### 4.2 Required uncertainty treatment

For synthetic or public-data MVPs, `q_a` and `κ` are assumptions, not measured facts.

They must therefore be:

- exposed in the assumptions ledger;
- adjustable in the interactive panel;
- propagated in the Monte Carlo;
- never described as measured.

Initial conservative defaults may be configured, but defaults must be visually marked as assumptions.

### 4.3 MVP simplification

The first demo may assume:

```text
q_a = q_0
```

unless the intervention plausibly changes checkout conversion after selection.

Example:

- feed completeness may change selection but not checkout conversion;
- a price cut plausibly changes both selection and checkout conversion;
- free shipping plausibly changes both.

If an action-specific `q_a` is not modeled, the system must state:

> “Post-selection conversion held constant across arms.”

---

## 5. Correction B — model cannibalization

An AI-attributed order is not necessarily an incremental merchant order.

Some customers might have bought through:

- direct;
- organic search;
- paid search;
- email;
- affiliate;
- marketplace;
- another merchant-owned surface.

Therefore agent-attributed demand must not automatically be valued as net-new demand.

### 5.1 MVP parameter

Add:

```python
cannibalization_rate: float  # [0, 1]
```

to merchant economics.

Default synthetic scenarios should sweep at least:

```text
0%
25%
50%
75%
```

The interactive report must show whether the top-ranked action remains top under plausible cannibalization.

### 5.2 Long-term replacement

Replace the prior using merchant measurement:

- incrementality testing;
- geo holdouts;
- switchbacks;
- matched cohorts;
- referral-path analysis;
- order-channel history where available.

Do not claim realized incremental revenue until this is measured.

---

## 6. Correction C — remove “random fair share” as a headline metric

Do not define:

```text
fair_share = 1 / number_of_products
```

and do not headline an index based on that number.

Twenty products are not equally suited to every mandate.

A 5% baseline therefore has no economic or causal interpretation merely because the candidate set contains twenty products.

### 6.1 Immediate replacement

Report:

```text
Controlled selection share: 1.5%
```

or:

```text
Selection rate across tested mandates: 1.5%
```

### 6.2 Later benchmark options

A legitimate relative index may later be built using:

- matched-peer subsets;
- category / price-segment expected share;
- clone experiments;
- neutralized-attribute experiments;
- estimated expected share conditional on mandate fit.

Until then, no “index 30” language.

---

## 7. Correction D — separate public product facts from synthetic merchant economics

The best immediate MVP should avoid fully fictional products if possible.

Use:

```text
PUBLIC / SOURCE-VERIFIED
    product titles
    prices
    dimensions / specifications
    visible shipping terms
    visible return terms
    visible structured attributes
    ratings if legally / operationally appropriate
```

and:

```text
SYNTHETIC / ILLUSTRATIVE
    COGS
    payment fees
    fulfillment cost
    return cost
    channel mix
    post-selection conversion
    cannibalization
    price elasticity
```

The report must label the distinction.

### 7.1 Important Shopify Global Catalog constraint

Shopify Global Catalog may be used as a **live discovery adapter or developer research tool**, but do not make it the persisted frozen experiment fixture without confirming current terms.

Current Shopify documentation explicitly restricts caching/reusing catalog search results and images.

Therefore the reproducible MVP fixture should be built from one of:

1. manually source-verified public product pages;
2. merchant-supplied CSV;
3. a dataset for which persistence is explicitly permitted;
4. manually entered normalized product facts with source URLs and timestamps.

Store no externally sourced image assets in the MVP unless usage rights are clear.

### 7.2 Canonical product schema

Keep an internal `ProductState` that is provider-neutral.

Map relevant fields to current agent-commerce feed semantics where useful.

As of this specification, OpenAI’s stable product feed explicitly includes fields for:

- price and availability;
- shipping service/cost/handling/transit timing;
- return acceptance and return deadline;
- ratings/reviews;
- product / variant attributes.

Do not make OpenAI’s schema itself the core domain model.

Instead:

```text
provider-neutral ProductState
        ↓
OpenAI feed projection
Shopify/UCP projection
other provider projection
```

This preserves cross-platform value.

# PART IV — EXPERIMENT SYSTEM

## 11. Units and pairing

Preserve the v2.0 design:

```text
experiment
└── intervention arm
    └── mandate
        └── replication
            └── probe
```

Treatment comparisons are paired at the mandate level.

For each mandate `m`:

```text
d_m
  = mean(Y | treatment, mandate=m)
    - mean(Y | control, mandate=m)
```

The estimator should operate on the distribution of `d_m`, not treat every raw probe as independent.

---

## 12. Candidate order control

For each `(mandate, replication)` pair:

1. generate one candidate-order seed;
2. shuffle candidates once;
3. reuse identical order for every treatment arm in the pair.

Candidate order should differ across pairs.

Execution order across all cells should be globally randomized.

Store:

```text
candidate_order_seed
candidate_ids_in_order
execution_sequence
```

in the run artifacts.

---

## 13. Prompt rendering

### Release 0

One deterministic prompt renderer.

### Release 1

Two semantically equivalent renderers.

### Release 2

Three renderers, with sign-stability / magnitude diagnostics.

The structured buyer mandate must remain canonical and independent of wording.

Example:

```python
@dataclass(frozen=True)
class BuyerMandate:
    mandate_id: str
    category: str
    budget_min_cents: int | None
    budget_max_cents: int | None
    urgency: str
    intended_use: str
    required_features: tuple[str, ...]
    preferred_features: tuple[str, ...]
    excluded_features: tuple[str, ...]
    context_tags: tuple[str, ...]
```

Renderer versions are metadata, not data mutations.

Required output:

# PART V — CONSTRUCT VALIDITY

## 18. The central scientific threat

Closed-catalog experiments may exaggerate the value of fields because the system makes those fields perfectly structured and salient.

This is especially dangerous if the fields that look best economically are also the fields most artificially emphasized by the laboratory.

Therefore the MVP must include one **representation-validity experiment**.

---

## 19. Representation contrast

Choose one fact, preferably shipping SLA.

Create three treatments with the same underlying commercial truth:

### Arm A — structured

The shipping promise appears in the dedicated structured shipping field.

### Arm B — natural text

The same promise appears naturally in product copy or a generic attribute string.

### Arm C — absent

The promise is not exposed.

All economic reality is held constant.

Measure:

```text
structured vs absent
text vs absent
structured vs text
```

Interpretation:

### Good signal

```text
structured > text > absent
```

or:

```text
structured ≈ text > absent
```

The commercial fact appears to matter beyond a single schema encoding.

### Warning

```text
structured >> text ≈ absent
```

The apparent lift may be mostly schema salience.

The report should label this as a lab-specific sensitivity.

This test is more important than adding several extra commercial arms early.

# PART VI — STATISTICS

## 20. Immediate estimator

Do not over-engineer Release 0.

At the mandate level:

```text
control_rate_m
treatment_rate_m
d_m = treatment_rate_m - control_rate_m
```

Estimate the ATE as:

```text
mean(d_m)
```

For intervals, the existing Bayesian bootstrap over mandate-level paired differences is acceptable for MVP use.

Equivalent implementation:

```python
# one d_m per mandate
weights ~ Dirichlet(1, ..., 1)
theta_draw = sum(weights[m] * d[m] for m in mandates)
```

Report:

```text
posterior mean
90% interval
95% interval
P(effect > 0)
number of mandates
raw probes
```

---

## 21. Do not rely on generic ICC planning after pilot data exists

The ICC / design-effect calculation is useful as a warning that replication does not equal independent information.

It should not remain the primary power calculator once real pilot results exist.

After 50–100 real mandates:

1. estimate the empirical paired-difference distribution;
2. resample/simulate experiments at candidate mandate counts;
3. estimate probability of resolving decision-relevant effects.

Build:

```python
def empirical_power_curve(
    paired_differences,
    mandate_counts,
    decision_threshold_pp,
    draws=5000,
):
    ...
```

Report:

```text
mandates needed for:
    80% probability of resolving 3 pp
    80% probability of resolving 5 pp
```

Spend additional budget on mandate breadth before repeated identical prompts.

---

## 22. Sequential execution

Sequential execution is allowed.

However Release 0 and Release 1 should prioritize operational simplicity over elaborate stopping machinery.

Recommended:

```text
run initial block
    50 mandates

if top actions are obviously separated:
    stop for demo purposes

if decision ordering remains ambiguous:
    add mandates in blocks of 25–50
```

For Validated MVP, formalize stopping using posterior and economic decision uncertainty.

Implement an explicit merchant economics object.

```python

    elasticity_max: float
```

Do not use “agent share of orders” as the only volume input.

Keep:

```text
comparison events
selection
post-selection conversion

```

as separate quantities.

---

## 24. Contribution margin

Preserve cent-safe arithmetic.

```text
CM(a)
  = net_price(a)
    - COGS
    - fulfillment_cost(a)
    - payment_cost(a)
    - shipping_subsidy(a)
    - expected_return_cost(a)
```

where:

```text
expected_return_cost(a)
  = return_rate(a) × return_cost
```

Use integer cents or Decimal.

Never use binary floating-point for monetary results.

---

## 25. Agent-channel value

```text
orders_agent(a)
  = N_cmp × s_a × q_a
```

```text
contribution_agent(a)
  = orders_agent(a) × CM(a)
```

```text
Δorders_agent(a)
  = orders_agent(a) - orders_agent(control)
```

```text
cannibalization_cost(a)
  = max(0, Δorders_agent(a))
    × κ
    × CM_displaced
```

```text
agent_channel_gain(a)
  = contribution_agent(a)
    - contribution_agent(control)
    - cannibalization_cost(a)
```

---

## 26. Non-agent effect

If intervention scope is `agent_only`:

```text
non_agent_effect(a) = 0
```

If `global`:

```text
non_agent_orders_after(a)
  = N_nonagent × [1 + volume_response(a)]
```

For price changes:

```text
volume_response(a)
  = -ε × Δprice_pct(a)
```

Be explicit about sign conventions.

Then:

```text
non_agent_effect(a)
  = non_agent_orders_after(a) × CM(a)
    - N_nonagent × CM(control)
```

For other global interventions, specify an action-specific cost/behavior model.

Do not invent hidden generic effects.

---

## 27. Channel scope replaces binary “isolable”

Use:

```text
agent_only
global
partial
```

rather than only a boolean.

For `partial`, add:

```python
spillover_fraction: float
```

Example:

An agent-exclusive promotion code may leak outside the agent channel.

Then model:

```text
non-agent exposure
    = spillover_fraction × relevant_non_agent_volume × intervention_cost
```

This is more realistic than pretending all levers are perfectly isolable or global.

---

```text
    cannibalization prior / range

ε_d

    if relevant

    if relevant
```

Then calculate:

```text

```

Report:

```text

```

Every uncertain input needs:

```text

measured vs assumed
```

### A. Structured attribute completion

# PART X — OPTIONAL RETRIEVAL ENVIRONMENT

## 35. Why it matters

Closed-catalog choice experiments measure:

```text
P(select | product is in candidate set)
```

Real agent commerce also depends on:

```text
P(retrieved)
```

Long-term total selection probability is closer to:

```text
P(retrieved)
× P(considered | retrieved)
× P(selected | considered)
```

The system should eventually distinguish these failure modes.

---

## 36. Minimal retrieval environment

Do **not** block Vertical Slice or Demo MVP on this.

When added:

```text
buyer mandate
    ↓
catalog retrieval
    ↓
top-k products
    ↓
buyer model
    ↓
selection
```

Report separately:

```text
retrieval rate
conditional selection rate
total selection rate
```

Use either:

- a local frozen catalog retriever;
- an explicitly permitted catalog API;
- a web-enabled environment with separate evidence class.

Do not mix retrieval treatment effects with closed-catalog choice effects in one number.

# PART XVI — COST CONTROL

## 60. Probe budget philosophy

The simulated oracle is for:

- structural debugging;
- statistical test debugging;
- report development;
- sensitivity engine testing.

Real providers are for:

- human-facing results;
- behavior measurement;
- cross-provider validation.

Do not spend real API calls to debug CSS, economics formulas, artifact paths, or treatment patching.

---

## 61. Output minimization

Default real probe output:

```text
one selected ID
one abstention flag
```

No long narrative.

This should materially reduce output cost.

---

## 62. Selective provider-B replication

Provider B need not duplicate the full matrix immediately.

Sequence:

```text
Provider A:
    broad arm screen

Provider B:
    control + economically important arms

If disagreement:
    expand targeted sample

If agreement:
    proceed
```

This creates useful cross-model evidence at lower cost.

---

## 63. Batch execution

For large offline evaluation runs, use provider batch APIs where current provider documentation and latency requirements make sense.

As of this specification:

- OpenAI Batch supports offline grouped processing and advertises a cost discount versus synchronous calls.
- Gemini Batch supports large-scale asynchronous evaluation at discounted pricing.

Implementation rule:

> Read official provider docs at implementation time. Do not hard-code assumptions about model IDs, pricing, schema syntax, batch availability, or turnaround.

The vertical slice should use synchronous requests for faster debugging.

Every merchant test should improve future intervention selection or calibration.

5. Before each ticket, state:
   - acceptance criterion;
   - files changed;

   - catalog version;
   - mandate version;
   - intervention version;
   - prompt version;
   - adapter version;
   - parser version;
   - provider;
   - model ID;
   - seeds.

11. Never convert unresolved output into the focal product.
12. Never use rationale as an outcome.
13. Never fabricate product facts for an “attribute completion” arm.
14. Never call a lab effect “real-world lift.”

17. Keep comparison events, selections, conversions, and orders distinct.
18. Explicitly model cannibalization.
19. All monetary arithmetic is cent-safe.
20. Any global intervention must include non-agent economics.
21. Any partially isolated intervention must expose spillover.
22. Every action keeps its operational requirements.

# PART XXV — FALSIFICATION GATES

## 80. Kill / pivot conditions

### Gate A — effects are mostly noise

If even large, commercially meaningful interventions cannot be distinguished with reasonable mandate counts:

```text
do not build SaaS
```

Investigate whether the product should shift toward feed diagnostics rather than causal optimization.

### Gate B — representation dominates reality

If effects appear only when facts are placed in artificially privileged structured fields:

```text
lab construct validity is weak
```

Build external/retrieval environments before making stronger claims.

### Gate C — merchant economics eliminate nearly all opportunity

If qualified merchants consistently show agent-mediated opportunity too small to justify spend:

```text
the current market may be too early
```

Do not hide this behind projected market growth.

### Gate D — merchants already know the answer

If recommendations collapse to obvious heuristics and merchants gain no new decision information:

```text
the wedge is insufficient
```

Increase intervention sophistication or reconsider the product.

### Gate E — lab ranking does not transfer

If intervention ranking is unrelated to live/external outcomes:

```text
the core predictive claim fails
```

The business may remain a controlled agent-readiness testing product, but the larger optimization thesis is weakened.

# PART XXVII — FINAL PRODUCT PRINCIPLE

## 84. The product must remain a decision system

The failure mode for this company is becoming:

```text
a sophisticated dashboard
showing many interesting agent metrics
```

The home screen should always end in:

```text
DO THIS
because:
    measured agent response = X
    modeled economic impact = Y
    uncertainty = Z

```

Every future feature should be evaluated by whether it makes the recommendation:

- more accurate;
- more calibrated;
- more merchant-specific;
- more executable;
- more defensible;
- more valuable.

The long-term company should not sell “AI visibility.”

It should sell:

> **merchant profit optimization for agent-mediated demand.**
