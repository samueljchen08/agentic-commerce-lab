The Wedge

The biggest AI‑visibility win is often the worst action on the board.

AI buyer agents compare a merchant against a frozen competitive set and choose one. This system runs controlled experiments on that choice, then runs the winning lever through real unit economics before it ever reaches a to-do list — because most levers can't be scoped to the ~3% of demand agents actually mediate. Below: a controlled demonstration of that gap, then two live runs against real Claude Sonnet 5 on a real, source-verified catalog.

01 — The demonstration

Agent-choice lift vs. modeled dollar value

E0 · SYNTHETIC SYSTEM CHECK

A controlled run on a simulated oracle — 150 mandates × 3 replications × 5 arms, 2,250 probes, baseline selection rate 12.0%, ICC 0.013. Large enough to resolve every effect cleanly. This is a pipeline validation and a demonstration of the mechanism, not a merchant fact — real numbers are in Section 02.

Free shipping produces the largest agent-choice lift and the largest loss. It is a global lever — it discounts every non-agent order too, and agents mediate only about 3% of this merchant's comparisons. The dollar model charges that exposure; a tool that only reports selection lift would recommend it anyway.

02 — Real-world validation

Two live runs, same spec, different winner

E1a · CONTROLLED LAB, SINGLE PROVIDER

Two independent runs against live claude-sonnet-5, identical Arbor pricing, identical checkout-verified 12-product catalog, 60 mandates × 5 arms × 1 replication (300 real probes, ~$2.85 each). They disagree on which lever wins.

This disagreement is the finding, not a bug. Both runs sit near a 5–7% baseline — 3 to 5 buyers out of 60 choosing Arbor at all. A single mandate flipping its answer swings an entire arm from a confident winner to statistical noise. Run v4 also predates a token-budget fix: 12 of its 300 probes hit the output ceiling mid-answer and were correctly dropped, never guessed — v5 is fully clean (0 of 300 dropped). Neither run is wrong; at n=60 the ranking simply isn't stable yet, which is exactly why the system reports a probability of a positive effect rather than a single number. More mandates, not more replications of the same ones, is what resolves it — see the assumptions ledger in Section 04.
